A little later than Preston, Alfred Marie Cornu became interested in the
Zeeman effect.713 He possessed a Rowland grating, presented to him by Henry

published his results on the magnetic influence on spectral lines in several notes,
submitted to the Academy of Sciences, Paris: in the first, which was presented on
18 October 1897, he reported only about triplets and their polarization, which he
found to agree with Lorentz' theory (Cornu, 1897); however, in the second,
presented on 17 January 1898, he corrected his earlier results, claiming in
particular that the sodium Z>2-line was split into a quadruplet and that the same

simultaneously with Cornu, Henri Becquerel also concerned himself with the Zeeman

molecular band lines of carbon, cyanide and nitrogen, Becquerel and Deslandres
found in no case any sign of the Zeeman effect.
