# Chapter III

Completion of the Matrix Scheme
Matrix mechanics was formulated at Göttingen during the University's summer
vacation in 1925. Born and Jordan presented the programme of the theory and
worked out its most obvious consequences in August and early September; they
were then joined by Werner Heisenberg, who, since the end of July, had taken a
complete leave from work on physical problems and had spent nearly a month
and a half in Munich and on hiking tours in the Alps.101 In September,
Heisenberg did not return to Göttingen; rather he went to Copenhagen, but from
there he contributed to the elaboration of the scheme of matrix mechanics in
letters to Born and Jordan. Two months later Born, Heisenberg and Jordan
submitted a long paper, entitled 'Zur Quantenmechanik. IL9 ('On Quantum
Mechanics IF), to Zeitschrift für Physik, which covered fifty-nine pages of its
volume 35 (Born, Heisenberg and Jordan, 1926; abbreviated as BHJ, 1926, in the
following).
It may seem remarkable how this long memoir containing the theory in its
most general form—including periodic systems of several, even infinitely many,
degrees of freedom—could be composed in such a short period of time,
especially since the three co-authors spent little more than a week together.102
However, several circumstances combined to yield fast progress of the work.
First, Heisenberg, on hearing about the early success of the matrix methods—
such as the proof of energy conservation—became very enthusiastic about
applying them himself. With his active participation Born and Jordan's
programme received a big push forward, for he immediately thought of using matrix
mechanics in those physical problems with which he had been concerned for
some time. Born and Jordan were gratified to have Heisenberg on their side, for
they could rely on his physical intuition. Second, in extending the matrix scheme
to general multiply periodic systems, all three of them were able to make use of
their thorough knowledge of the classical theory, especially of the perturbation
schemes and the theory of canonical transformations. Hence they experienced no
